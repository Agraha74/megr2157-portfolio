
# A11 – Motor Selection Design Part 2

## My Part: Frame Analysis and Design

## Objective

The objective of this portion of the assignment was to design and analyze the structural frame of a 2 kN tensile testing machine. This included selecting mounting brackets, analyzing the horizontal frame, and verifying the strength and stability of the vertical frame members.

The frame must withstand the forces generated by the two leadscrews and the torque produced by the two motors while maintaining a minimum safety factor of 4.

## Design Requirements

- Maximum tensile load: 2,000 N
- Load per leadscrew: 1,000 N
- Number of motors: 2
- Number of leadscrews: 2
- Motor torque: 1.02 N·m per motor
- Safety factor: 4
- Crosshead speed: 1–50 mm/min
- Leadscrew length: 24 inches
- Frame material: Heavy 40 Series aluminum extrusion

## Decide

Heavy 40 Series aluminum extrusion was selected for the proposed frame because of its structural capabilities, modularity, ease of assembly, and compatibility with T-slot mounting brackets.

Reinforced corner brackets were selected to provide additional structural support at the connections between the horizontal and vertical frame members.

The final component selections will be verified using the completed analytical calculations and finite element analysis.

## Mistakes and Design Changes

During the initial vertical frame simulation, the applied forces were located too close to the fixed geometry. This resulted in unrealistically low stress and displacement values. The loading locations were corrected so that the forces were applied at the unsupported end of the frame. Another issue identified during the simulation was that the assigned material had a yield strength of 620.4 MPa. This value must be verified against the actual material specifications before completing the final analysis.

## Lessons Learned

Throughout this assignment, I learned how to analyze structural frame members subjected to multiple types of loading. I gained a better understanding of how motor torque creates reaction forces throughout a structural assembly and how these forces can be used to select mounting components.

I also learned the importance of applying the correct boundary conditions and material properties in SolidWorks Simulation. Incorrect loading locations can significantly affect simulation results and produce misleading factors of safety.
