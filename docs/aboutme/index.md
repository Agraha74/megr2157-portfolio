About Me


Hello, my name is Andrew Graham. I am in my 3rd year here at UNC Charlotte. I am a mechanical engineering student with a strong interest in motorsports and hands-on engineering. I was drawn to mechanical engineering because I have always been interested in understanding how mechanical systems work and how individual components come together to create a complete system. That interest has developed further through my involvement with Formula SAE, where I have been exposed to areas such as vehicle systems, wiring, powertrain integration, data acquisition, and general race car development. I enjoy engineering most when I can take something from an idea or design and apply it to a real system where performance can be measured and improved.

As I continue developing as an engineer, I want to become someone who can combine mechanical design with practical problem-solving. Motorsports is especially interesting to me because small design decisions can have a noticeable effect on performance, reliability, and usability. I want to continue improving my ability to analyze a problem, develop possible solutions, and then use testing and engineering principles to determine which solution works best. My goal is to become an engineer who understands both the theory behind a design and how that design performs in the real world.

What It Means to Defend an Engineering Decision

To me, defending an engineering decision means being able to explain why a design choice was made using evidence instead of simply saying that it seemed like the best option. That evidence could include calculations, testing, physical principles, cost, safety, or comparisons with other designs. At this point, I understand the basic idea of defending an engineering decision, but I still have a lot to learn about doing it thoroughly. I can explain some of the reasoning behind my choices, but I want to become better at supporting those decisions with calculations, models, testing, and clear engineering evidence.
